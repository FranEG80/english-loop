# Resumen

El future in the past permite hablar de algo que era futuro desde un punto de
referencia pasado. Usamos `would` para expectativas o predicciones, `was/were
going to` para intenciones y planes, `was/were about to` para inminencia y
`was/were due to` para horarios o compromisos previstos.

`She said the parcel would arrive on Friday.`
`They were going to move, but the offer fell through.`

# Objetivos

- Relatar predicciones y expectativas vistas desde el pasado.
- Describir planes pasados, tanto realizados como cancelados.
- Expresar que una acción estaba a punto de suceder.
- Referirse a eventos programados desde una narración en pasado.

# Explicación

## `Would` como futuro visto desde el pasado

`Would + infinitive` presenta una predicción o una expectativa posterior a un
momento pasado: `In 2019, experts thought the bridge would open in 2021.` No
afirma por sí solo que la apertura ocurriera; solo recoge la predicción desde
ese punto de vista.

## `Was/were going to`

Usamos `was/were going to + infinitive` para planes o intenciones que existían
en el pasado: `I was going to call you, but I lost your number.` El plan puede
haberse realizado, cancelado o quedado sin confirmar; el contexto lo aclara.

## `Was/were about to` y `was/were due to`

`Was/were about to + infinitive` indica que una acción era inminente: `The
concert was about to begin when the power failed.` `Was/were due to + infinitive`
presenta un horario o compromiso: `The minister was due to arrive at noon.`

# Forma o estructura

- `subject + would + base verb`.
- `subject + was/were going to + base verb`.
- `subject + was/were about to + base verb`.
- `subject + was/were due to + base verb`.
- En estilo indirecto, `will` suele retroceder a `would`: `He said, “I will
  return”` → `He said he would return`.

Después de `would`, `going to`, `about to` y `due to` usamos la forma base:
`would leave`, `was going to leave`, `was about to leave`, `was due to leave`.
El auxiliar `was/were` concuerda con el sujeto.

# Usos principales

1. **Predicción pasada:** `The guide said the path would take two hours.`
2. **Expectativa o resultado posterior:** `We knew the news would surprise her.`
3. **Plan o intención pasada:** `They were going to renovate the kitchen.`
4. **Plan cancelado o interrumpido:** `I was going to complain, but the manager apologised.`
5. **Acción inminente en una narración:** `The match was about to start.`
6. **Horario o compromiso pasado:** `The train was due to leave at 06:15.`

# Contrastes importantes

## `Would` frente a `was going to`

`Would` suele transmitir una predicción, una expectativa o el futuro de una
declaración pasada: `She knew the job would be demanding.` `Was going to`
presenta una intención o plan del sujeto: `She was going to apply for the job.`

## `Was going to` frente a `was about to`

`Was going to` puede referirse a un plan lejano o general. `Was about to` sitúa
la acción inmediatamente antes de ocurrir: `We were going to leave in June`,
pero `We were about to leave when the alarm sounded`.

## `Was due to` frente a `would`

`Was due to` se apoya en un horario o compromiso: `The ferry was due to depart
at nine.` `Would` expresa lo que alguien esperaba o predijo: `The captain said
the ferry would depart at nine.`

# Ejemplos

1. **The architect believed the tower would withstand strong winds.**
2. **I was going to send the invoice that afternoon.**
3. **The audience was about to applaud when the actor returned.**
4. **The parcel was due to arrive before lunch.**
5. **They knew the final decision would affect every department.**
6. **We were going to cycle home, but the weather changed.**
7. **The goalkeeper was about to take the penalty.**
8. **The committee was due to meet in September.**

# Errores frecuentes

- ❌ *She said the train will arrive at six.*  
  ✅ **She said the train would arrive at six.**  
  En estilo indirecto, el futuro suele retroceder a `would`.

- ❌ *They were going renovate the flat.*  
  ✅ **They were going to renovate the flat.**  
  `Going` necesita `to` antes del infinitivo.

- ❌ *I was about leaving when you called.*  
  ✅ **I was about to leave when you called.**  
  `About to` lleva infinitivo, no gerundio.

- ❌ *The bus was due leaving at 7:20.*  
  ✅ **The bus was due to leave at 7:20.**  
  `Due to` lleva la forma base.

- ❌ *He would to help us.*  
  ✅ **He would help us.**  
  Después de `would` no usamos `to`.

# Excepciones relevantes

`Would` también puede expresar hábitos pasados o cortesía, no solo futuro en el
pasado. En `Every summer, we would camp by the lake`, el significado es un
hábito repetido. El contexto distingue ese uso del de `She said we would camp
by the lake`, que es una expectativa futura desde el pasado.

`Was/were due to` puede hablar de un evento que finalmente no ocurrió: `The
flight was due to leave at eight, but it was cancelled`. La expresión conserva
el horario previsto, no garantiza que el evento se realizara.

# Mini resumen

- `would + verb` = futuro, predicción o expectativa desde un punto pasado.
- `was/were going to + verb` = plan o intención pasada.
- `was/were about to + verb` = acción que estaba a punto de suceder.
- `was/were due to + verb` = horario o compromiso previsto.
- En todas las formas, el verbo principal va en forma base.

# Comprobación rápida autocorregible

1. She said the package ___ arrive on Tuesday. (will)  
   - Respuesta: `would`  
   - Explicación: El futuro de una declaración pasada suele retroceder a would.
2. We ___ going to book the cottage, but it was already full. (be)  
   - Respuesta: `were`  
   - Explicación: We concuerda con were.
3. The speaker was about ___ the results. (announce)  
   - Respuesta: `to announce`  
   - Explicación: About to lleva infinitivo.
4. The ferry was due ___ at 05:45. (leave)  
   - Respuesta: `to leave`  
   - Explicación: Due to presenta un horario previsto.
5. I was going ___ the supplier after lunch. (call)  
   - Respuesta: `to call`  
   - Explicación: Going to introduce una intención pasada.
